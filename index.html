What survived
the noise.

Years of reading, listening, watching, studying, wandering through Reddit and following whatever seemed worth opening have left behind a strange collection of patterns. I don't have a complete record of any of it. This is an attempt to capture what I can still use.

Ideas→Make→Keep learning

Some are half-formed. Some may be wrong. The useful ones are the ideas I keep coming back to.

01 / OBSERVATIONS

Ideas I keep noticing

Ideas that became more useful when they showed up in more than one place.

01 / SYSTEMS

Technology rarely removes a bottleneck. It moves it.

Cheap printing shifted scarcity toward distribution. Cheap internet shifted it toward attention. Cheap AI production may shift it toward verification, trust and selection.

What I take from it
When something becomes abundant, look for the thing that has become scarce because of that abundance.
02 / PEOPLE

Incentives can explain behaviour better than intentions.

People can sincerely value one thing while repeatedly behaving according to another. The structure around a person often tells you what behaviour will survive.

What I take from it
When trying to understand a system, ask what it rewards—not just what it says it values.
03 / HISTORY

Geography creates constraints that politics cannot simply wish away.

Trade routes, rivers, mountains, coastlines, climate and access to resources repeatedly shape what states can do over long periods.

What I take from it
Before analysing a political choice, understand the physical constraints around it.
04 / SYSTEMS

Systems can produce outcomes nobody intended.

Millions of individually sensible decisions can combine into congestion, bubbles, misinformation, fragility or other collective outcomes.

What I take from it
The intention behind an institution explains its origin; it does not necessarily explain its eventual behaviour.
05 / TECHNOLOGY

When information becomes abundant, judgement becomes scarce.

The problem used to be finding something worth reading. Increasingly, the harder problem is deciding which of the thousands of things available deserve attention.

What I take from it
Curation isn't about collecting more. It's about reducing the search space without losing the important signal.
06 / PEOPLE

People don't encounter information in a vacuum.

Identity, status, fear, memory, belonging and prior beliefs affect how the same piece of information gets interpreted by different people.

What I take from it
Understanding a claim often requires understanding the person and context through which the claim is being received.
07 / SYSTEMS

What gets measured starts getting optimised.

A metric begins as a proxy for a goal. Once rewards are attached to it, the proxy can quietly become the goal itself.

What I take from it
Whenever a metric becomes a target, ask what important thing it might stop representing.
08 / HISTORY

Logistics often decides what grand strategy can actually accomplish.

Empires and armies require food, transport, money, supply chains, infrastructure and disease control. Ideas operate inside physical constraints.

What I take from it
When an outcome looks surprising, inspect the boring infrastructure underneath it.
09 / PEOPLE

Reputation is accumulated information.

A strong reputation reduces the amount of verification required before people cooperate, transact or trust.

What I take from it
Trust is not merely an emotion; it can reduce transaction costs.
10 / TECHNOLOGY

Scale changes the problem, not just the quantity.

A process that works for ten people may fail at ten thousand because communication, coordination, incentives and failure modes change.

What I take from it
Scaling isn't always “more of the same.” Sometimes it creates a new class of problem.
11 / PEOPLE

People can be sincere and still be wrong.

Confidence and sincerity tell us something about conviction. Neither establishes whether the underlying belief is correct.

What I take from it
Intellectual humility is less about doubting everything and more about keeping beliefs updateable.
12 / SYSTEMS

The intermediary becomes valuable when complexity rises.

When there are too many products, sources, opinions or choices, someone who can reduce search cost and add context becomes useful.

What I take from it
The value of a filter increases with the amount of noise surrounding the decision.

03 / UNCERTAINTY

What trading taught me.

Not a trading strategy or signals. Just a few ideas about risk, uncertainty and decisions that I find useful outside a chart too.

01 / ASYMMETRY

Before upside, understand the downside.

It is easy to get attracted to what something could return. The harder question is what happens if the assumption is wrong.

What I take from it →
I find myself asking this before committing too much money, time or attention to something. The upside matters, but so does the cost of being wrong.
02 / PROCESS

A good decision can still lose.

Sometimes you can make a sensible decision with the information available and still get a bad outcome. That's uncomfortable, but it is different from making a bad decision.

What I take from it →
I try to judge the thinking separately from the result. Otherwise a lucky outcome can teach me the wrong lesson, and an unlucky one can make me abandon a good process.
03 / PROBABILITY

Uncertainty doesn't mean doing nothing.

You rarely know what happens next with certainty. You can still make a decision based on what seems more likely, while knowing what would make you reconsider it.

What I take from it →
This is useful to me outside markets because waiting for certainty can become an excuse for never acting.
04 / FEEDBACK

Failure is useful only if you learn the right thing from it.

A loss can mean the idea was wrong. It can also mean the timing was wrong, the conditions changed, or the outcome was simply unlucky.

What I take from it →
Before changing everything after a failure, I want to know what actually failed. Otherwise I may be correcting something that wasn't broken.
05 / SAMPLE SIZE

A streak is evidence, not proof.

A few wins can make almost any process look convincing. A few losses can do the opposite.

What I take from it →
I try to be careful about turning a short run of success into a story about skill. The same applies to businesses, habits and people.
06 / SURVIVAL

Don't make one mistake fatal.

Being right matters less if being wrong once can remove your ability to make the next decision.

What I take from it →
In business and life, I like decisions that leave room for another attempt. Staying in the game is an advantage in itself.
07 / RISK

Knowing the boundary can make action easier.

It sounds counterintuitive, but defining what you are willing to lose can reduce the fear around taking a reasonable risk.

What I take from it →
I don't need to know everything before starting. I need to know whether I can live with being wrong.
08 / REALITY

Conviction is not evidence.

You can understand something deeply, explain it confidently and still have the world disagree with you.

What I take from it →
Strong belief can help you commit, but it shouldn't be confused with proof. Reality gets the final vote.
09 / LUCK

Being right doesn't prove your reasoning was right.

An outcome can validate a conclusion by accident. That makes success surprisingly dangerous: it can reinforce a bad explanation.

What I take from it →
When something works, I want to separate “it worked” from “I know why it worked.” Those aren't always the same thing.
10 / OPTIONALITY

Not acting is sometimes the better decision.

There are situations where the best move is to keep your options open rather than force a decision just because you can.

What I take from it →
I've become more comfortable with passing on things when the conditions aren't good enough. Opportunity cost works both ways.
11 / LONG RUN

You don't have to win every round.

A probabilistic approach can tolerate being wrong repeatedly. What matters is whether the overall process survives and compounds over enough attempts.

What I take from it →
This changes the question from “How do I make sure I'm right?” to “How do I build a process that can keep working even when I'm wrong sometimes?”
12 / THE ONE I KEEP

I'm less interested in being right than being able to survive being wrong.

This is probably the idea from trading that travels furthest for me — into money, business, careers, relationships and even ideas.

Why it stayed →
You don't need to predict everything correctly. You need enough room to learn when you don't.
04 / LINES

Lines that made me stop

Not a collection of favourite quotes. Just words that made me stop, rethink something or remember an idea.

“If you look at a thing with desire, you never look at the thing as it is.”
Osho
What stayed with me is that wanting something can change what I think I'm seeing. That's useful to remember with people, ambition, money — basically anything I badly want to be true.
“The business of human wellbeing is the only real business. All other businesses are subsidiaries of that.”
Sadhguru · The Fundamental Business of Human Wellbeing
I don't read this as saying business should become charity. I read it as a test of what a business ultimately creates for the people it serves — and whether profit is the result of creating value or the only thing being optimised.
“In an age of nearly infinite leverage, judgment is the most important skill.”
Naval Ravikant · Judgment Is the Decisive Skill
Leverage makes a decision travel further. That means better tools don't remove the need for judgment; they increase the cost of having poor judgment.
“You took the standards as being Real. The norms. The beliefs. The societal notions. The expert talk. The intellectual jargon. All that is in the culture, you took to be Real.”
Kapil Gupta, M.D. · Questions From Long Time Private Clients
The useful question for me isn't whether society is always wrong. It's whether I've mistaken familiarity for truth simply because an idea is repeated often enough and delivered with enough authority.
“The first principle is that you must not fool yourself.”
Richard Feynman · Cargo Cult Science
The part I find useful is that the person most capable of fooling me is me. Confidence in an explanation isn't evidence that the explanation is true.
“He who has a why to live can bear almost any how.”
Viktor Frankl · Man's Search for Meaning
What interests me is the relationship between meaning and endurance — not the sentence as a motivational slogan.
Editorial rule: a line earns its place only when there is an idea behind it that I can actually unpack. Quotes are checked against reliable sources; uncertain wording is treated as a paraphrase.
06 / ABOUT

Why this exists

The list is incomplete on purpose.

I'm not trying to prove that I've read a lot.

I've read quite a few books, watched a ridiculous number of films and shows, listened to podcasts, gone through articles, spent too much time in Reddit rabbit holes and followed whatever looked interesting enough to open.

I don't have a clean list of all of it. I don't remember every book I've finished, and I definitely don't remember every useful thing from the books I do remember. There are probably dozens of things that influenced how I think without me being able to trace them back to the source.

So I don't want this to become a bibliography dressed up as a personality.

What interests me is the residue: what stayed, what changed how I look at something, and what I still find myself using.

Some of these notes will be incomplete. Some will probably be interpretations rather than the author's original point. And some will change as I learn more.

That's fine. I'd rather have an honest, growing record of what I actually remember and use than pretend I have a perfectly reconstructed history of everything I've consumed.
